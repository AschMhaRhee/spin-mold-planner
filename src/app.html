Rules the planner uses

Condensed from Oster Pewter's Principles of Centrifugal Rubber Mold Casting (chapters 8–10). Read the original for the reasoning and drawings.

How a cavity fills

  • Spin pushes metal to the outer end first; the cavity fills from the outside in. The side nearest the centre fills last, so that's where air gets trapped and where most flaws show up.

Placement

  • Balanced, symmetrical layout. Put heavier pieces opposite each other and as close to the centre as practical.
  • Beginner clearances: at least 2¾" from centre and ⅝" from the rim. Experienced moldmakers go closer.
  • Large, thin, flat designs distort under spin. Inspect castings before using one as a model for the next mold.

Gates

  • Gate into the thickest section, which shrinks most and freezes last. Never gate through a thin feature (a jump ring, a sword tip). It freezes first and cuts off the heavy section.
  • Prefer a flat surface and an inconspicuous spot. Use a narrow, tapered channel that ends in a thin slot.
  • Use as few gates as possible, ideally one. Multiple gates cause cold shuts.
  • Start small and enlarge after trial passes; you can't make a gate smaller. Short-pasty-range alloys need larger gates.
  • Pour considerably more metal than the cavities need, so the sprue and gates feed shrinkage.

Vents

  • Main vent: on the line from the centre through the cavity's centre, at the wall nearest the centre.
  • Direct-gated cavities: one vent on each side of the ingate at 45°.
  • Also vent narrow appendages that trap air (ears, legs, tails, points), spots that don't fill after warm-up spins, and spots that keep showing porosity.
  • Pie-wedge vent: three shallow scalpel cuts make a small triangle whose tip just touches the cavity wall. Then cut a shallow channel slightly toward the centre, turn sharply, and zig-zag out to the rim. Straight channels clog.
  • If gates are in the way, needle-drill through to the back of the mold half and channel to the rim on the back.

When a cavity won't fill: the fix order

  1. Fix the venting.
  2. Top-gate (cut a matching gate in the top half).
  3. Enlarge the gate.
  4. Don't add gates.

To check whether venting is the problem, reverse the spin direction for a few passes. If the flaw moves, the venting is at fault.

Help improve these rules

This planner is open source, and the rules get better when casters share what happened. If a layout worked, or failed, post a casting report with photos and your settings. Think a rule is wrong? Suggest a change. The full rule list, and where each rule lives in the code, is in docs/RULES.md.

What this tool can't know

  • Your machine speed, clamp pressure, mold temperature and rubber compound all change the result. Suggested gate sizes are starting points, sized from the part's thickness and width at the gate and the metal's gate factor. Always trial cast.
  • Miniatures (fully 3D parts) are laid out by their top-view footprint. The tool splits nothing between mold halves, so plan the parting line yourself.
